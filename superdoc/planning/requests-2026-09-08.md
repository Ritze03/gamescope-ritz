stop just buys 41 % rather than 100 %, and the lever for *that* is `GAMMA_MIN`, deliberately
untouched here.

has no such curve. New `halfsplit` scene (left half the dark scene's 5..20 bands, right half
the bright scene's 200..255): the frame's p98 comes from the bright half, the gain settles at

moves which half is wrong.

**What was built: Local adaptation**, one new param, 0..100 %, default 50, Dynamic only
(Whole image is untouched, and the host masks the value to 0 there).
`cs_effects_measure.comp` writes a **16×16 map of smoothed local mean luminance** into rows
1..16 of the history texture (now 16×17) — for **zero extra taps**, because the pass is 16×16
threads and thread (tx,ty) already owns exactly the 8×8 tap block covering image cell (tx,ty),
so the sum it computes for the mean's tree reduction *is* that cell's mean. It is smoothed
spatially by a separable binomial blur at strides 4,2,1,1 (à-trous, variance 22, σ ≈ 4.7 of
16 cells) and temporally by the same asymmetric EMA every other statistic gets.
`cs_effects_layer0.comp` samples it bilinearly by hand (the texels are float bits over four
UNORM8 lanes, so hardware filtering would interpolate *bytes*), forms
`r = clamp(local/mean, 0.25, 4)`, blends it as `r^strength`, and feeds `p98·r`, `p50·r`,
`p2·r` into the **same** curve. Strength 0 makes `r` exactly 1.0, so it is the old path
bit-for-bit; and because the curve still clamps afterwards, a locally-adapted pixel can never
leave the user's `[min_gain, max_gain]`.

## [x] 13. The ceiling: Target brightness above 0.5 and Max gain above 2.0 do nothing

> "It still feels like anything above target brightness 0.5 and max gain 2.0 does anything
> at all" (read with the missing negation: *raising* either one appears to do nothing).

This follows item 11, which measured the applied gain reaching 3.0 and 4.0 on the flat
dark band chart and concluded the knob worked. It does on that chart. It does not on a
frame that looks like a game.

**Measured first, on the CPU, from the same `effects_curve.h` text the GPU compiles, over
seven scenes x seventeen targets x four Max gain settings** (harness kept out of the repo;
its dark-scene numbers reproduce the GPU capture code-for-code, which is what makes it
trustworthy). Two independent ceilings, one per slider:

* **Target** reached the picture only through the exponent `g = ln(target) / ln(p50*G)`,
  clamped to a fixed `[0.5, 1.5]`. Solving `g = 0.5` gives `target = sqrt(p50*G)`: every
  target above that is the identical clamped gamma. Where that threshold sat, at the
  shipped defaults: dark band chart **0.436**, textured dark scene **0.461**, a dark
  photographic frame **0.394**, a night-city photograph **0.581**. The default target 0.5
  is on the dead side of three of those four.
* **Max gain** only ever fed `clamp(0.9/p98, min, max)`, and a realistic frame has enough
  bright content that `0.9/p98` is small: **2.05** on the dark photographic frame, **2.83**
  on the textured dark scene, **2.38** on the night-city one. Above that the clamp never
  binds and the slider is an **exact** no-op. The flat 5..20-code chart hid it: its p98 is
  0.15, so it asks for 5.9 and every setting up to 4.0 bites -- which is exactly the table
  item 11 published.

**Proved on the GPU, before and after, with the old binary and the new one** (two full
`scripts/effects-regression.sh` runs, `adaptive-ceiling-2026-09-08-OLD` and
`adaptive-ceiling-2026-09-08`). Textured dark scene, frame mean:

| sweep | OLD | NEW |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 (Max gain 4) | 83.0 / 112.1 / **112.1** (step +0.0) | 82.8 / 129.4 / 175.4 (+46.6 / +46.0) |
| Max gain 1.5 / 2 / 3 (Target 0.5) | 90.0 / 103.1 / 112.1 (+13.1 / +9.0) | 65.7 / 103.0 / 129.4 (+37.3 / +26.4) |
| Max gain 3 vs 4 (Target 0.5) | 112.06 vs **112.06** -- byte-identical | 129.39 vs 129.39, and the panel now says why |

**The fix**, in `src/shaders/effects_curve.h` -- two changes, each needed for one half:

1. `gamma_min = clamp(1/max_gain, 0.25, 1.0)` instead of a fixed 0.5, so Max gain governs
   "how bright may it go" in both channels -- the exact mirror of min_gain, which already
   means "how dark may it go" through the shadow cap. **max_gain 2.0 reproduces the old
   0.5 floor exactly**, which is why 2.0 is the anchor and why every pre-existing measured
   number is still reproducible; max_gain 1.0 now really does not brighten, where before
   the gamma still lifted a 20-code band to 71.
2. The gain also carries `target^(1/gamma_min) / p50` -- the exposure the gain must supply
   so the gamma, *at its floor*, can still land the median on the target -- so past the
   floor the gain takes over and Target keeps moving the picture. `Why that and not the
   simpler target/p50:` the simple form is a bigger gain, and moving lift out of the toe
   into a linear gain darkens the shadows at the same median. Measured: it dropped the
   half-dark/half-bright scene's dark half from 12/17/23/28/34 to 6/9/13/18/22. Taking the
   smallest gain that leaves the target reachable leaves that scene bit-identical.

**What it costs.** Bright, mid, half-split and both halo scenes are **bit-identical** to
the old binary's captures (checked by diffing the two runs' `results.txt`: those lines do
not appear in the diff at all). The dark chart at the defaults gets brighter -- 71/90/111/
128/143 -> 88/107/127/143/157, rect 254 and pure black 0 unchanged -- because the gamma
the median asks for (0.417) is no longer clamped up to 0.5. Stability is unchanged or
better: still-frame peak-to-peak **0** at Local adaptation 0 / 50 / 100, and the panning
gain spread fell 0.0324 -> 0.0191. Halo amplitude identical (+8 / +15, -7 / -14).

**The residual ceiling, stated plainly.** Once the mid-tones ARE on Target, Max gain stops
being a brightness control and becomes a contrast one -- the median is pinned, so a higher
Max gain puts more of the same level into the linear gain and less into the toe (dark
chart at target 0.5: max_gain 3 gives 93/152 for inputs 5/20, max_gain 4 gives 84/157).
On the textured dark scene at the default target, Max gain 3 and 4 are still the same
picture. That is *correct*, and the point of the third change:

3. **`ab_dyn_binding()` and the "adaptive limit" fact.** A clamped slider looks exactly
   like a working one; that is what cost the session. The Shaders area's Pipeline row now
   carries one short live line naming the binding constraint, classified by the same
   function `effects_ab_log` prints, from the frame the Inspector's preview already
   captures. Captured on screen at three settings: "none -- the mid-tones are on Target
   brightness", "Max gain -- Target brightness does no more here" (at target 0.9, and
   again at max gain 1.5).
